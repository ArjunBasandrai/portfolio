<section>

## The task

Semantic segmentation is per-pixel classification: rather than asking "is there a bird," the model labels every pixel as bird or background, producing a precise mask of the animal. I trained a U-Net to do exactly that on bird photographs.

</section>

<section>

## Dataset

CUB-200-2011 (Caltech-UCSD Birds-200-2011) is a standard benchmark for bird vision work: 11,788 images across 200 species, with species labels, bounding boxes, and crucially for this project, segmentation masks. The masks are what make supervised segmentation possible here.

</section>

<section>

## Model

U-Net is the workhorse of image segmentation. Its encoder-decoder shape, joined by skip connections, is what lets it produce high-precision masks: the encoder uses convolutions and max-pooling to compress the image into high-level features, the decoder uses transposed convolutions to rebuild a full-resolution mask, and the skip connections carry fine spatial detail across from encoder to decoder so it is not lost in the bottleneck.

</section>

## Results

On in-domain images, North American birds like those in CUB-200-2011, the model segments cleanly, reaching a validation BCE loss of **0.1193**.

<Callout label="The interesting failure">
The model segments North American birds well, but hand it a species from the Indian subcontinent, outside the CUB-200 domain, and the masks fall apart. A clean demonstration of domain gap: the network learned the birds it saw, not "birdness" in general.
</Callout>

The out-of-domain results, on Indian subcontinent species the model never trained on, are visibly worse.

<section>

## Where it could go

This was a quick build on a plain U-Net, so the obvious next step is attention gates, which let the decoder focus on the relevant regions of the image instead of weighting everything equally. They are a natural fit for the domain-gap problem above: a model that attends to bird-shaped structure rather than memorized appearance should generalize better to species it has not seen.

</section>
