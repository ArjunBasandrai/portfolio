<section>

## The problem

On a chest X-ray, the ribs and clavicle sit directly over the lungs, and their shadows hide exactly the soft-tissue detail a radiologist needs. Bone shadow suppression is the task of removing those overlapping bone structures while leaving the lung detail intact, effectively turning a standard X-ray into a soft-tissue image without a second exposure.

</section>

<section>

## Dataset

I trained on the augmented JSRT and BSE-JSRT datasets from [Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/hmchuong/xray-bone-shadow-supression): more than 4,000 chest X-rays, each paired with a version where the rib and clavicle shadows have been removed. That paired structure makes this a supervised image-to-image problem, the model learns the mapping from "with bone shadows" to "without."

</section>

<section>

## Model

I implemented the recent [xU-NetFullSharp](https://github.com/xKev1n/xU-NetFullSharp) architecture. It builds on U-NetSharp and uses bidirectional multi-scale skip connections, in the spirit of U-Net3+, so features flow between encoder and decoder at every scale rather than only at matching depths. It also swaps plain ReLU for the more modern xUnit activation, which learns a spatial activation map rather than applying a fixed pointwise nonlinearity.

<Figure src="https://cdn.hashnode.com/res/hashnode/image/upload/v1737031302009/9a262c0f-295a-4e96-abfd-80f54873b07f.jpeg" alt="xU-NetFullSharp architecture" caption="xU-NetFullSharp: bidirectional multi-scale skip connections with xUnit activations." />

The full model is large, so the implementation lives in the [training notebook](https://github.com/ArjunBasandrai/xU-NetFullSharp-Bone-Shadow-Suppression/blob/main/notebooks/xu-netfullsharp-training.ipynb) rather than inline here.

</section>

<section>

## Loss

I trained on an L1-SSIM loss, a weighted blend of pixel-wise L1 (mean absolute error) and a structural similarity term. L1 keeps the per-pixel intensities honest, while SSIM rewards preserving local structure and contrast, which is what actually matters for keeping lung detail legible.

<Equation label="L1-SSIM, a + b = 1">loss = a · L1 + b · (1 − SSIM)</Equation>

Optimizing for structure as well as raw pixel error is what pushes the reconstructions past the smeared look that pure MSE training tends to produce.

</section>

<section>

## Results

The trained model reaches **0.987 SSIM** and **37.39 dB PSNR** on the test set, and more importantly it reconstructs the finer detail inside the lungs that the bone shadows had been hiding.

<Callout label="What worked">
Suppressing the ribs without smearing the lung tissue is the hard part. The structural loss term is what kept the fine vascular detail intact while the bone shadows came out.
</Callout>
